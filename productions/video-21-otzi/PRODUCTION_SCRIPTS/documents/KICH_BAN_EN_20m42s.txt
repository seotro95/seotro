ÖTZI: THE LAST JOURNEY, STILL UNFINISHED
English narration — planned timeline 20:42; audio timings verified separately.

001 | 00:00:00.000–00:00:10.000
A stone point, smaller than a finger, remained inside a man's shoulder for more than five thousand years. It changed his story.

002 | 00:00:10.000–00:00:20.000
The man had climbed into the Alps carrying food, tools, and a wounded hand. Someone shot him from behind. What happened next?

003 | 00:00:20.000–00:00:30.000
We can investigate his final journey. But the closer we look, the more important one question becomes: which parts can we actually know?

004 | 00:00:30.000–00:00:40.000
His modern name is Ötzi. His own name, his reasons for travelling, and the identity of the person who attacked him remain unknown.

005 | 00:00:40.000–00:00:50.000
This is a reconstruction built from a body, a meal, damaged equipment, and microscopic traces. Each clue has a different kind of clock.

006 | 00:00:50.000–00:01:00.000
Our task is to put those clocks together without pretending they all tell the same time, or turning a possibility into a memory.

007 | 00:01:00.000–00:01:10.000
The modern story begins on September nineteenth, nineteen ninety-one. Two hikers noticed a body emerging from ice near the border of Italy and Austria.

008 | 00:01:10.000–00:01:20.000
At first, a recent mountain accident seemed plausible. Recovering ancient evidence was not the original plan, and the earliest rescue attempts caused damage.

009 | 00:01:20.000–00:01:30.000
Then the objects changed the question. A copper axe, plant-fibre cord, and unfamiliar clothing belonged to a world far older than expected.

010 | 00:01:30.000–00:01:40.000
Radiocarbon dating placed him in the Copper Age, roughly around thirty-three hundred BCE. The ice had preserved a person, not an anonymous legend.

011 | 00:01:40.000–00:01:50.000
He was an adult man, probably in his forties. His preserved body offers unusually intimate evidence, but preservation also changes what investigators can measure.

012 | 00:01:50.000–00:02:00.000
Drying, compression, recovery damage, and later storage must be separated from injuries he suffered while alive. Even a remarkably complete body is an altered archive.

013 | 00:02:00.000–00:02:10.000
Place the discovery on the landscape: the Tisenjoch area, more than three thousand metres above sea level, in the high Ötztal Alps.

014 | 00:02:10.000–00:02:20.000
The modern border is useful for finding the site. It cannot tell us his nationality. He lived thousands of years before these countries existed.

015 | 00:02:20.000–00:02:30.000
Nor should we imagine an empty wilderness below him. Copper Age communities farmed, kept animals, made tools, and maintained contacts across difficult terrain.

016 | 00:02:30.000–00:02:40.000
Ötzi carried products of that skilled world. His journey connects valley life with mountain routes, although the purpose of this particular crossing remains uncertain.

017 | 00:02:40.000–00:02:50.000
Was he escaping trouble, travelling between communities, or doing something more ordinary before violence intervened? The equipment cannot choose one explanation by itself.

018 | 00:02:50.000–00:03:00.000
Start with what the landscape can record. A traveller passes through different plant communities as elevation changes. Some of their traces enter the body.

019 | 00:03:00.000–00:03:10.000
Pollen grains are tiny, durable structures. Under a microscope, their shapes can help specialists identify the plants that produced them, sometimes very precisely.

020 | 00:03:10.000–00:03:20.000
But pollen is not a location tracker. Wind moves it. Food and water carry it. A useful interpretation needs ecology, context, and independent evidence.

021 | 00:03:20.000–00:03:30.000
Investigators studied plant remains in different parts of Ötzi's digestive tract. Those samples offered a sequence of environments rather than a continuous recording.

022 | 00:03:30.000–00:03:40.000
Published reconstructions suggest movement between lower, wooded terrain and higher alpine ground during his final days. They do not establish every turn he took.

023 | 00:03:40.000–00:03:50.000
Hop-hornbeam pollen helped connect part of that journey to woodland south of the main ridge. Seasonal plant evidence points toward the warmer part of the year.

024 | 00:03:50.000–00:04:00.000
That matters because the ice can mislead our imagination. A body found in snow did not necessarily die during a midwinter blizzard on a frozen continent.

025 | 00:04:00.000–00:04:10.000
Spring or early-summer conditions can still be dangerous high in the mountains. Sunshine in a valley says little about wind and exposure on a ridge.

026 | 00:04:10.000–00:04:20.000
The itinerary is therefore a working reconstruction. It gives us plausible movement through ecological zones, not a schedule with verified departure and arrival times.

027 | 00:04:20.000–00:04:30.000
Now add the clue that makes this more than a story of difficult travel: a deep injury between the thumb and index finger of his right hand.

028 | 00:04:30.000–00:04:40.000
The wound was sustained while he was alive, before the arrow injury. Researchers commonly place it in the preceding days, although exact timing remains approximate.

029 | 00:04:40.000–00:04:50.000
A defensive injury is one interpretation. But an injury's position cannot tell us the words exchanged, the number of people present, or who began a confrontation.

030 | 00:04:50.000–00:05:00.000
Picture the practical consequence instead. Climbing, gripping a shaft, preparing food, and repairing equipment all become harder when the dominant hand is painful or impaired.

031 | 00:05:00.000–00:05:10.000
That does not make every damaged tool evidence of a chase. Objects wear out in ordinary life, and some changes can happen after death.

032 | 00:05:10.000–00:05:20.000
Ötzi's kit included a small stone dagger, a tool for retouching stone edges, a copper axe, a quiver, and an unfinished wooden bow stave.

033 | 00:05:20.000–00:05:30.000
Several arrow shafts were unfinished. A bow stave is not automatically a ready weapon, and a quiver's contents do not guarantee immediate hunting or defence.

034 | 00:05:30.000–00:05:40.000
Studies of the stone toolkit identified heavily used edges and a limited supply of workable material. Skilled maintenance mattered when fresh stone was not readily available.

035 | 00:05:40.000–00:05:50.000
A wounded hand and incomplete equipment make a difficult combination. Together they support questions about his situation, but they still do not prove a pursuit.

036 | 00:05:50.000–00:06:00.000
The axe adds another scale to the story. Analyses of its copper connect the metal with sources in what is now Tuscany, far to the south.

037 | 00:06:00.000–00:06:10.000
That result indicates long-distance connections. It does not mean Ötzi personally travelled to the mine, or that we know every exchange along the way.

038 | 00:06:10.000–00:06:20.000
An object can move through many hands. Its materials may reveal a network while leaving the owner's status, occupation, and personal itinerary unresolved.

039 | 00:06:20.000–00:06:30.000
Why was such a valuable object still nearby? Robbery seems an incomplete explanation. Yet possessions left behind cannot establish the attacker's motive or identity.

040 | 00:06:30.000–00:06:40.000
The temptation is to turn every object into a plot point. A better question is simpler: what could this equipment actually help a traveller do?

041 | 00:06:40.000–00:06:50.000
His clothing answers part of that. Hides, fur, plant fibres, and carefully worked seams formed a practical system, assembled from different materials for different jobs.

042 | 00:06:50.000–00:07:00.000
Genetic work on the surviving garments identified several animal sources. This was not a uniform costume made from one convenient hide, but a collection of choices.

043 | 00:07:00.000–00:07:10.000
The shoes combined an outer structure, an inner net, and insulating grass. Their exact performance cannot be read from appearance alone, but the design is sophisticated.

044 | 00:07:10.000–00:07:20.000
Insulation works by slowing heat loss. Dry material holds pockets of air; wet material behaves differently. A mountain traveller must manage both warmth and moisture.

045 | 00:07:20.000–00:07:30.000
Walking produces heat, but it also demands energy. Stop moving, add wind, or become injured, and equipment that seemed adequate can face a different problem.

046 | 00:07:30.000–00:07:40.000
His birch-bark containers carried another clever possibility: transporting embers. Charcoal and plant remains connect a fragile container with the practical management of fire.

047 | 00:07:40.000–00:07:50.000
That evidence does not prove a fire was burning beside him at the moment of attack. Capability and action are different claims, even when a reconstruction looks convincing.

048 | 00:07:50.000–00:08:00.000
These objects make him recognisably human: prepared in some ways, vulnerable in others. To approach the final hours, however, we need a more immediate record.

049 | 00:08:00.000–00:08:10.000
The next clue was inside his stomach. Its unusual position in the preserved body delayed recognition, reminding researchers that ancient anatomy can be difficult to read.

050 | 00:08:10.000–00:08:20.000
When investigators finally studied the contents, they found a substantial last meal. The dying traveller had eaten, rather than simply reaching the mountain in empty-handed desperation.

051 | 00:08:20.000–00:08:30.000
Microscopy and molecular analyses identified ibex, red deer, cereals associated with einkorn, and traces of bracken. Different methods contributed different pieces to that reconstruction.

052 | 00:08:30.000–00:08:40.000
The high proportion of fat was especially striking. In an active traveller's food, fat provides concentrated energy without requiring the weight of an equally energetic low-fat meal.

053 | 00:08:40.000–00:08:50.000
This is evidence about a meal, not a complete dietary biography. One stomach sample cannot tell us what every Copper Age household ate throughout the year.

054 | 00:08:50.000–00:09:00.000
Nor do the bracken traces settle their own purpose. Food wrapping, accidental ingestion, and possible medicinal use have been discussed. Deliberate treatment remains a hypothesis.

055 | 00:09:00.000–00:09:10.000
The degree of digestion suggests he ate relatively shortly before death. But digestion depends on physiology and food, so it is not an exact stopwatch.

056 | 00:09:10.000–00:09:20.000
A substantial meal can suggest a pause. It cannot tell us that he felt safe. People under pressure still eat, rest, and make practical decisions.

057 | 00:09:20.000–00:09:30.000
Here is the first version of our timeline: earlier movement through wooded environments, an injured right hand, a climb, and food eaten before the end.

058 | 00:09:30.000–00:09:40.000
The order is meaningful, but the gaps are large. We do not yet know whether the meal came before the arrow wound, or how far he moved afterward.

059 | 00:09:40.000–00:09:50.000
To reduce that uncertainty, investigators turned to the shoulder. In two thousand and one, an arrowhead was identified in the body, transforming interpretations of his death.

060 | 00:09:50.000–00:10:00.000
An entry wound lay on the upper left side of his back. The point had penetrated the shoulder region. This was clear evidence of an arrow injury.

061 | 00:10:00.000–00:10:10.000
Later imaging linked the wound to damage near the left subclavian artery, a major blood vessel. The injury was serious; its consequences required closer interpretation.

062 | 00:10:10.000–00:10:20.000
For years, a familiar explanation followed: a shot from behind caused rapid fatal bleeding. It was compelling, and it gave the case a dramatic final scene.

063 | 00:10:20.000–00:10:30.000
But a plausible mechanism is not the same as a measured survival time. New analyses have returned to the scans and asked what the tissues actually show.

064 | 00:10:30.000–00:10:40.000
A twenty twenty-five forensic study created three-dimensional models from earlier CT data. It examined the wound, surrounding structures, and a preserved collection of blood.

065 | 00:10:40.000–00:10:50.000
The authors estimated roughly one hundred and ten millilitres for that reconstructed collection. Crucially, this was not a direct measurement of all blood he ever lost.

066 | 00:10:50.000–00:11:00.000
They cautioned that external bleeding could not be excluded. Drying and compression also complicate reconstruction. The result challenged certainty about immediate death without solving the entire case.

067 | 00:11:00.000–00:11:10.000
A separate clinical reassessment published later in twenty twenty-five considered how the wound channel would behave in a living shoulder, before the tissues shrank.

068 | 00:11:10.000–00:11:20.000
It argued that movement of muscle could limit external bleeding and that survival for hours was possible. Exertion, altitude, and cold might then become important.

069 | 00:11:20.000–00:11:30.000
These are research interpretations, not recovered footage. We should neither declare that he died instantly nor replace that certainty with a claim that he definitely walked for hours.

070 | 00:11:30.000–00:11:40.000
The distinction changes the geography of the story. If he survived and moved, the place where the body was found might not be where the arrow struck.

071 | 00:11:40.000–00:11:50.000
The arrow's direction also needs care. The position of a dried, compressed body is not automatically its posture when an injury occurred thousands of years earlier.

072 | 00:11:50.000–00:12:00.000
Forensic modelling can reposition bones and test alignment. It can suggest compatible postures, but it cannot recover every movement, slope angle, or position of the shooter.

073 | 00:12:00.000–00:12:10.000
The attacker remains absent from the surviving evidence. A flint point has no signature that names its owner, and a direction of travel does not identify a motive.

074 | 00:12:10.000–00:12:20.000
Even the missing arrow shaft invites competing explanations. Removal, breakage, body movement, and later processes must be considered before assigning a deliberate act to someone.

075 | 00:12:20.000–00:12:30.000
A confident murder scene would be easy to film. A scientifically honest scene leaves room around the evidence, because the missing information is part of this case.

076 | 00:12:30.000–00:12:40.000
What about a final blow to the head? Earlier studies discussed head trauma, raising the possibility of a fall or further violence after the arrow wound.

077 | 00:12:40.000–00:12:50.000
The later twenty twenty-five clinical paper questioned whether clear evidence established a traumatic brain injury during life. That disagreement belongs in any current reconstruction of the ending.

078 | 00:12:50.000–00:13:00.000
We therefore cannot present a pursuer striking him as a confirmed event. Nor can we confidently choose a fall. Both would add actions the evidence does not settle.

079 | 00:13:00.000–00:13:10.000
The right hand deserves the same caution. A proposed bandage might help explain its preserved position, but the original wrapping and its application are not directly witnessed.

080 | 00:13:10.000–00:13:20.000
A useful reconstruction separates three levels: the object or injury observed, the mechanism proposed, and the human story imagined around it. Those levels should remain visible.

081 | 00:13:20.000–00:13:30.000
Let us test the popular escape story. A hand wound suggests earlier trouble. Incomplete equipment suggests practical limitations. The arrow provides direct evidence of violent injury.

082 | 00:13:30.000–00:13:40.000
Together, these clues make conflict a reasonable line of investigation. They do not reveal a continuous chase from a village to the mountain pass.

083 | 00:13:40.000–00:13:50.000
The axe still lying nearby does not rule out every form of robbery. An attacker might leave quickly, overlook an object, or have another purpose altogether.

084 | 00:13:50.000–00:14:00.000
A full stomach does not rule out danger either. And an unfinished bow does not prove that he expected none. Everyday decisions rarely fit one tidy explanation.

085 | 00:14:00.000–00:14:10.000
The harder question is whether several independent clues support the same sequence, and whether another sequence could also fit them without inventing new evidence.

086 | 00:14:10.000–00:14:20.000
One possibility places a rapid fatal attack near the final resting place. Another allows injury elsewhere followed by further movement. Current evidence does not give us a complete route.

087 | 00:14:20.000–00:14:30.000
What survives is a bounded history: he travelled, ate, carried specialised equipment, suffered injuries, and died in the high Alps. The links are less certain than the points.

088 | 00:14:30.000–00:14:40.000
That uncertainty does not make the science weak. It tells us where an observation ends, where a model begins, and which discoveries could still change the account.

089 | 00:14:40.000–00:14:50.000
There is another complication. The mountain continued acting on the evidence long after the traveller died. Ice preserved him, but preservation need not mean perfect stillness.

090 | 00:14:50.000–00:15:00.000
An influential early account imagined a protected hollow that kept the body safe beneath ice for millennia. Later work has re-examined that simple time-capsule picture.

091 | 00:15:00.000–00:15:10.000
A twenty twenty-two reappraisal argued that melting, exposure, refreezing, and movement could help explain the body's surroundings and the distribution of objects found nearby.

092 | 00:15:10.000–00:15:20.000
That interpretation concerns what happened after death. It does not erase the arrow injury, but it warns against treating every object's final position as a frozen photograph of an attack.

093 | 00:15:20.000–00:15:30.000
A tool's position may reflect a traveller's action, later environmental movement, recovery, or several processes combined. Archaeology must investigate those alternatives rather than choose the most cinematic one.

094 | 00:15:30.000–00:15:40.000
This is why documentation matters. Early photographs, excavation records, material studies, and new imaging can answer different questions about the same small patch of mountain.

095 | 00:15:40.000–00:15:50.000
The site is not just a backdrop. It is part of the evidence, and part of the uncertainty, connecting the final journey with thousands of years of environmental change.

096 | 00:15:50.000–00:16:00.000
Now return to the living man. Reconstructions often give him thick flowing hair and a familiar rugged appearance. Even that image has needed revision as methods improved.

097 | 00:16:00.000–00:16:10.000
A higher-quality genome published in twenty twenty-three supported darker skin and a genetic tendency toward male-pattern baldness, along with strong ancestry related to early Anatolian farmers.

098 | 00:16:10.000–00:16:20.000
Genes do not deliver a complete portrait. They inform probabilities and ancestry, while hairstyle, expression, individual experience, and much of personal identity remain beyond their reach.

099 | 00:16:20.000–00:16:30.000
He also carried evidence of an active, physically demanding life and health problems. Such findings add context, but no single diagnosis can stand in for his final experience.

100 | 00:16:30.000–00:16:40.000
His tattoos have inspired discussion about treatment and pain. Their presence is observable; their exact meaning and the intentions behind each mark are harder to recover.

101 | 00:16:40.000–00:16:50.000
A copper axe does not make him a chief. A bow does not make him a full-time hunter. A mountain death does not make his whole life an escape.

102 | 00:16:50.000–00:17:00.000
The more personal the evidence becomes, the more carefully we should resist giving him an invented biography. A person was larger than the objects that happened to survive.

103 | 00:17:00.000–00:17:10.000
Imagine assembling the case on a table. The hand injury belongs before death. The stomach contents preserve food. The arrow point preserves an event of bodily harm.

104 | 00:17:10.000–00:17:20.000
Each item is concrete. The spaces between them contain the difficult questions: when, where, how quickly, and under what circumstances did one event follow another?

105 | 00:17:20.000–00:17:30.000
The pollen sequence may constrain an itinerary. Digestion may constrain a meal's timing. Wound analysis may constrain survival. None supplies an exact shared clock.

106 | 00:17:30.000–00:17:40.000
A strong account looks for agreement across these methods. It also records their assumptions, because apparent precision can disappear when a model's starting conditions change.

107 | 00:17:40.000–00:17:50.000
For example, a blood-volume estimate from a dried mummy requires reconstruction. The result matters, but its uncertainty must travel with the number whenever we explain it.

108 | 00:17:50.000–00:18:00.000
And a landscape route inferred from plants relies on where those plants grew, how their traces entered the body, and how digestion moved them through it.

109 | 00:18:00.000–00:18:10.000
This is not a contest between a dramatic story and no story. The evidence itself offers a powerful narrative: repeated attempts to ask better questions of the same material.

110 | 00:18:10.000–00:18:20.000
In nineteen ninety-one, an ancient traveller emerged from the ice. A decade later, the arrowhead changed the case. New imaging keeps changing what investigators can test.

111 | 00:18:20.000–00:18:30.000
In twenty twenty-six, a museum-led project brought detailed surface documentation and internal CT information together in a digital twin, extending opportunities for study without repeatedly handling the mummy.

112 | 00:18:30.000–00:18:40.000
A digital twin is a research tool, not a resurrection. It can preserve dimensions and relationships while still leaving motive, memory, and final thoughts beyond measurement.

113 | 00:18:40.000–00:18:50.000
Better documentation can reveal new relationships and monitor fragile material. It also lets future researchers revisit today's interpretations with techniques that do not yet exist.

114 | 00:18:50.000–00:19:00.000
That makes the ending open in a specific way. We may learn more about his wounds or preservation, while some questions about the attacker may remain permanently unanswered.

115 | 00:19:00.000–00:19:10.000
We should not fill those gaps with a child's face, a grieving family, or a named enemy. None of those images is needed to understand the surviving evidence.

116 | 00:19:10.000–00:19:20.000
What we can show is the material world around an adult life: stitched hides, grass inside shoes, sharpened stone, food carried uphill, and a carefully hafted axe.

117 | 00:19:20.000–00:19:30.000
These details are not decoration. They explain capability, effort, and vulnerability. They make the journey tangible without requiring us to pretend that we witnessed the final confrontation.

118 | 00:19:30.000–00:19:40.000
So what happened in Ötzi's final hours? We can place food, movement, injury, and a high-mountain death within a limited sequence, supported by different kinds of evidence.

119 | 00:19:40.000–00:19:50.000
We can say that an arrow entered from behind and seriously injured his left shoulder region. We cannot give a verified minute-by-minute account of what followed.

120 | 00:19:50.000–00:20:00.000
We cannot name the person who fired it. We cannot establish every motive. And current debate cautions against treating either instant death or prolonged survival as settled fact.

121 | 00:20:00.000–00:20:10.000
The most responsible reconstruction therefore ends with a boundary. Here is the evidence. Here are the interpretations it supports. Beyond that, the mountain keeps its silence.

122 | 00:20:10.000–00:20:20.000
Return to that small stone point. It transformed an apparent mountain accident into a case of violence, but it did not transform uncertainty into complete knowledge.

123 | 00:20:20.000–00:20:30.000
Ötzi's story is compelling because one human life left so many traces, and because each generation of researchers has had to reconsider what those traces can mean.

124 | 00:20:30.000–00:20:40.000
The last journey is unfinished in our understanding. That is where this investigation ends today: with a person, a mountain, and questions still worth asking.

125 | 00:20:40.000–00:20:42.000
